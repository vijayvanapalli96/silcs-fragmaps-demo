About This Demo

🔬 P38 MAP Kinase

P38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (p38 MAPK) is a critical enzyme involved in cellular stress responses, inflammation, and immune regulation. It plays a key role in diseases including rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, and cancer, making it an important drug target. The crystal structure shown here (PDB: 3FLY) captures the ATP-binding pocket in the kinase domain, including the conserved hinge region and the DFG motif — key features for inhibitor design.

💊 Crystal Ligand & Poses

This demo includes the crystallographic ligand from PDB 3FLY along with 30 additional ligand poses refined using SILCS-MC (Monte Carlo). SILCS-MC optimizes ligand placement by sampling poses that overlap favorably with FragMap densities, producing "posref" (pose-refined) coordinates. This aligns ligand functional groups with the most energetically favorable binding regions.

🗺️ What Are SILCS FragMaps?

SILCS FragMaps are 3D probability maps generated from molecular dynamics simulations with small probe molecules (water, benzene, propane, etc.). They reveal where specific chemical functional groups prefer to bind on a protein surface, providing a "blueprint" for rational drug design.

💡 Why FragMaps Matter

FragMaps enable medicinal chemists to: identify binding hotspots before synthesizing compounds, optimize lead compounds by positioning functional groups in favorable regions, predict relative binding affinities (LGFE scores), and understand the energetic contributions of different molecular interactions.

FragMap Types & Colors

Each FragMap represents favorable binding regions for specific chemical functional groups. Negative GFE (Grid Free Energy) values indicate favorable binding — for example, an iso-value of -1.2 kcal/mol highlights regions where a functional group gains significant binding energy. More negative values represent stronger, more localized hotspots. The exclusion map (EXCL) uses positive values to mark regions sterically inaccessible to ligands.

Interpreting FragMaps

🎯 What to Look For

Toggle on HBDON (blue) and HBACC (red) maps in the binding site — you should see donor/acceptor hotspots near the hinge region where most kinase inhibitors form key hydrogen bonds. The APOLAR (green) map should show hydrophobic density in the back pocket, aligning with the aromatic/hydrophobic portions of the ligands.

🔍 Iso-value Guidance

Start with iso-values around -1.0 to -1.5 kcal/mol for GFE maps. Lower (more negative) thresholds show only the strongest hotspots. Raising the iso-value reveals broader, weaker interaction regions. For the EXCL map, higher positive values (e.g., +0.5) mark areas where ligand atoms cannot be placed due to steric clashes with the protein.

How to Use the Viewer

  • Rotate: Left-click and drag to rotate the structure
  • Zoom: Use mouse wheel to zoom in/out
  • Pan: Right-click and drag to pan the view
  • Select Ligands: Use the Ligand tab to switch between 31 different ligand poses

  • Adjust Iso-values: Use sliders to change the contour threshold for each FragMap
  • Focus Binding Site: Click "Zoom" to center view on the active ligand
  • Reset View: Click "Reset to defaults" to restore original settings
